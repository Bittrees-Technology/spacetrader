01 / Exchange

From base value to a quoted price

Storage fill sets scarcity; geography adds a modest logistics adjustment and the supply tier sets the spread. The public baseline repeats for the same item, station and cycle day; actual trade quotes also depend on stock. All tables here use generic reference examples.

Textiles, refined alloy, life-support filters and magnesium use an acyclic manufacturing floor. The reference value is at least ceil((sum(recipe input × generic local ask) + normal processing services + 1) / (1 − spread) × exp(impact × 0.5 / minimumDepth)). This retains the manufacturing reference and its conservative rounding allowance. The actual output bid still follows stock fill; this floor does not promise profitable processing at every fill level. It uses public dated baseline quotes, without a captain’s stock changes or private premiums. Reversible essential utility chemistry instead receives a station appropriation equal to any actual input-and-service shortfall after all coproduct receipts; the payment cannot be claimed by a captain.

G is the location diagnostic used by the modest logistics overlay. Seasonal stock changes move along the same centered curve; the seasonal and reserve diagnostics are not added again as price multipliers. Whole-credit rounding can make cheaper goods appear stepped.

05 / Local economy

Production, consumption and seasonal reserves

Gross production and gross consumption determine trade direction; net surplus is not an eligibility gate. A station can manufacture a good and still consume more than it produces. A factory may buy an ingredient without offering it back as retail merchandise. The same policy feeds the live exchange, visited commodity directory and generic catalogue. Buy-price columns describe player purchases; sell-price columns describe player sale proceeds.

Each station has its own size, delivery lead time, exports, inputs, imports and transit goods. Seasons represent procurement and production schedules, including at airless stations.

The reviewed 348-process capacity solution uses complete mass recipes and explicit alternate recovery routes. Coefficients are physical 250 kg lots, independent of value tier and inventory scaling. Intermediate mean output is 10,000 lots/day and higher-tier mean output is 3,000, supported by 20,000 mean primary collection lots. Gross repeated-stage output is 33,000, not 33,000 newly extracted lots. Ores and brines have no artificial household final-use sink. Each station has eight unrestricted core goods with at least five mean net-surplus lots/day under full supply; real inventories and inputs still limit sales and output.

Whole-day integrals use peaks at 600.5 and 720.5 in continuous time. All process capacity schedules use a common phase; the old sector-offset calendar is no longer active. Final demand retains the 120-day lag, without forcing factory inputs onto an unrelated wave. The 12,000–28,000, 6,000–14,000 and 1,800–4,200 limits are planned full-supply outputs. Actual disrupted production may fall below them. Every ingredient is reserved from opening stock plus arrivals; progressive filling redistributes shared inputs when a competing line is blocked elsewhere. Newly produced goods cannot feed another plant until the following daily tick. Outputs and useful coproducts are credited once, with waste recorded separately. Electrolysis oxygen is now real saleable coproduct stock, not a second free oxygen batch. Household final use and client-delivery credits are settled only once. See all process recipes.

06 / Inventory

Stock, bonding curves and freight

Every received shipment has a matching earlier source withdrawal. Freight stays in transit for the full route delay. A verified blockade suspends departures and holds arriving goods in transit until the route reopens; an unconfirmed statistical report does not destroy cargo. Missing inputs curtail factory output; households cannot consume absent goods. Ordinary routes have no invented random losses. Restricted dealers, player cargo losses and discarded process by-products remain separate, explicit channels.

Fleet trades, warehouse trades and ordinary client pickups use the same physical station/product pool. There is no independent reserve reset or unlimited restock. Surplus production continues to accumulate if routes close; sales are rejected at the storage safety limit rather than silently deleting overflow. Earlier v43, v44, v45, v47, v48 and v50 v2 market books retain their exact physical stock and freight on upgrade, including compact pipelines and shipments on closed routes. New capacity affects subsequent production, never owned cargo or cash. New commodity opening reserves are recorded separately as introduced stock; they do not refill the existing32goods or invent an old shipment. Only market deviations and losslessly compressed pending shipments are saved; R1 runs and the v51 D1 dictionary preserve exact Float64 quantities, with bounded station, good and route counts. Reading the board never advances time. Existing v1 exchange receipts retain their old evolution through migration day; owned cargo, basis, contracts and money are preserved.

The S-curve is centered at half of each station/product’s storage capacity. Equal stock fractions either side of 50% have equal and opposite deviations from the center price before rounding and spread. Empty storage reaches the upper reference limit, and full storage reaches the lower limit. Operating reserves still guide procurement and commercial needs, independently of this price midpoint. Seasonal production, use and shipments move the physical stock along this one curve; no seasonal price shock is added on top.

Explore the full centered stock-price curve. The chart runs from 0 to Max with a dashed 50% midpoint. Quantities are 250 kg net lots; ship holds, packaging and manufacturing ratios retain their physical meaning. Each lot clears at its own marginal half-lot price, so split orders settle identically and reversing a purchase crosses the spread. Liberation premiums remain capped below the ask at the matching sale midpoint. Real shipments can close price gaps; later production, use and replenishment can reopen them. Route expenses determine actual profit.

Physical station storage

Each station/product has a stable storage ceiling: max(100 lots, ceil(1.25 × highest planned seasonal inventory)). Production tapers above 90% occupancy including committed inbound shipments, and every batch must fit all coproducts before consuming ingredients. Incoming cargo reserves room, full stores defer unloading, and new dispatch cannot exceed uncommitted receiving space. Exchange sales, Max and Sell all respect physical room and the remaining daily buying allowance. The allowance is two days of mean consumption plus one day of net export production, multiplied by inventory scale and a coverage factor clamped from .05 to1.5. Signed procurement reserves its receiving quantities. Buying and selling cannot reset the same-day counters. These finite commercial budgets do not refill or remove physical goods. Existing excess is retained and worked down through use and outgoing trade.

Ordinary client freight

Acceptance removes each product from available source stock, loads compatible active holds, and freezes terms. Clients consume deliveries: destination exchange stock is unchanged at handover. Delivered lots offset subsequent local final-use demand, so the receiving client is not counted twice; this does not offset industrial recipe inputs. Client cargo cannot be sold, donated or counted as collateral. Late, lost or abandoned freight follows the signed restitution and breach terms. Surviving cargo stays sealed client property with no owned purchase basis. Only explicit theft converts retained cargo into owned goods after full restitution and the breach fee are billed.

Warehouse tenant leases

Canteen introductions offer fixed 14-, 30- or 60-day terms. Tenant goods occupy real space but remain outside owned inventory, collateral and net worth. Capacity, goods, terms and the offer’s 30-day cycle determine availability; no player save is used by this reference.

Interest accrues before that day’s bill. 10 / Catalogue

Baseline cycle lows, highs and local balance

These generic daily snapshots describe undisturbed public seasonal reserves. Player shipments and local liberation premiums can move actual voyage quotes outside this baseline range. No active voyage, private inventory or trading strategy is imported into the reference distributions.

Maximum gain, loss and quote frequency

Amounts are per traded unit and exclude fuel, provisions, fees, repairs and losses in transit. Percentages use the purchase price for that outcome. If all price pairs lose money, maximum gain is 0; if all pairs profit, maximum loss is 0. Missing buy or sell prices give N/A.

Quote frequency assumes two independent, uniform selections from the matching exchange station/day observations across the complete 1,440-day phase cycle. Each station/day counts once; buy observations include only stations that list the item, while sell observations include all accepting exchanges. This measures exact quoted extrema, not the probability of making a profitable journey. Actual journeys have ordered dates, travel costs, access and finite stock.

Typical gains, losses and their probabilities

The Return columns selector changes the leading value and sorting basis for all four return columns. Choose Credits first or Return % first, then click a gain/loss heading to sort. Switching basis preserves the selected column and direction. Both values remain visible and in CSV exports.

The average columns describe ordinary winning and losing quote pairs, using all daily price observations rather than exceptional top/bottom 10% tails. Each station/day counts once across the complete repeating cycle. Frequently quoted prices receive proportionally more weight. The displayed percentage is the average individual return on purchase price, not a return calculated from mean prices.

The frequency below each average is its share of all quote pairs, including break-even pairs in the denominator. Gain, loss and break-even probabilities sum to 100%. If a population contains no winners or no losers, that conditional average is N/A with 0% of pairs. Unsupported markets and missing buy/sell populations show N/A without a probability.

These are exact results for the stated model of two independent, uniformly selected exchange station/day observations. They are not a forecast for a particular route, ship or trading strategy. Departures, travel times, stock, licences and running costs are excluded. Traveller and restricted-dealer availability has no defined weight here, so those channels are excluded. Weighted histograms and cumulative sums calculate the results without allocating billions of quote pairs.

Daily buy and sell price distributions

The final column pools one daily quote per station across every day of the 1,440-day pricing cycle, separately for purchases and sales. Buy quotes include stations listing that good; sell quotes include all accepting exchanges. Each available station-day has equal weight. Matching market-access and item filters determine which exchange rows contribute. Histograms retain the exact count at every quoted price, so a price quoted on 100 days contributes 100 observations.

The solid cyan curve is Buy; the dashed gold curve is Sell. Both use the same horizontal credit-price scale and vertical frequency scale within that good. The Return columns selector affects gain/loss columns only; price curves always use credits. Each side is normalized to its own quote count. A sell-only selection still has a sell curve, with no fabricated buy observations. Intermittent travellers, restricted dealers and equipment offers have no daily availability distribution and contribute no invented observations.

Hover, focus or click markers for the exact minimum, maximum, mean, median and ±1, ±2 and ±3 population standard deviations, plus the share of daily quotes at or below/above that price. The two shares can overlap at tied prices. A low buy percentile helps identify an unusually cheap purchase quote; a high sell percentile identifies an unusually high bid. These frequencies describe public prices, not transaction volume or the probability of making a trade.

The drawing uses 128 common price bins, light mass-conserving local smoothing and shape-preserving cubic interpolation. It is an empirical price-frequency curve, not a fitted bell curve. Means, medians, extrema and deviations use the unsmoothed integer quotes. No density extends beyond a side’s observed minimum or maximum. Deviation markers beyond quoted support are labeled outside the quoted range; negative theoretical markers sit at the zero-price edge. Axis limits may extend beyond the observed quotes to accommodate those markers.

Holding goods allows you to choose when to sell. These curves show how frequently prices occur over a full cycle; they do not preserve timing order or estimate the wait until a target price returns. Travel, stock, access, storage, bank interest and other carrying costs still affect the result. The existing gain/loss amounts and their frequencies remain separate independent quote-pair comparisons. Their random-pair probabilities are not the success odds of a patient trading strategy. Repeating additional years verifies the recurring model but adds no new independent pricing phases.

For the current repeating seasonal model, checking every distinct daily phase covers an unlimited run. The pricing cycle lasts 1,440 game days, so integer daily phases repeat every 1,440 days. A calendar game year remains 365.25 days for annual interest and operating costs.

Net balance is mean production minus consumption multiplied by the item’s physical inventory scale. It excludes incoming and outgoing shipments. Positive means surplus, negative means consumption. A net magnitude below 0.000001 units/day is shown as N/A.

Item summaries apply visible filters across matching stations. If ordinary exchange rows are present, their daily quotes supply all four extrema, gain/loss statistics and curves. Private dealer envelopes remain visible in station rows; a private-only selection has its own range with no invented daily probability. Each of the four extrema is independent: the lowest buy and highest sell may occur in different places and on different days. Unavailable prices remain N/A.

Restricted-dealer bounds enumerate the 70 permitted price multipliers and stock/spread possibilities; they are a formula envelope, not a guarantee every joint combination is visited. Traveller, equipment and antiquity rows use their own formulas above. Baseline supply prices exclude eligible captain, fleet and service discounts.

12 / Relics & crew

Relic leads, collectors and named crew

Discovery uses a return journey and one work day. Raider-recovery work uses an Osprey reference, three work days, catalogue relic value as risk exposure and the existing 10% risk premium; only new defensive victories in ordinary travel count. Public service uses the local one-day Kestrel work budget and requires wider reputation 20 and local trust 10. These relic leads grant one named instrument, with no additional cash reward and no expiry. Each named relic can be granted free once per voyage; selling does not reset that provenance. Private canteen relic jobs also have no expiry; unrelated freight, passengers, transport and missions retain their deadlines. Formerly completed or failed outcomes stay historical.

Collectors at Meridian, Shackleton, Kepler, Occator, Callisto, Kraken and Triton use the same 45% relic bid as travelling antiquarians. Traveller prices, finite visit stock, all commodity quotes and all quote-pair distributions remain unchanged. Physical antiquity cases retain their condition, appraisal, ownership and custody rules.

Four named crew may be retained, with two on duty and at most one on-duty specialist per skill. The strongest crew or relic skill bonus applies, up to effective level 10, and never counts as earned XP or access qualifications. On-duty Specialist, Veteran and Elite quartermasters offer 5%, 10% and 15% on newly accepted freight-office fees. Only the strongest crew or relic premium applies; existing contracts stay fixed. The original four skill roles follow the effective-skill targeting, incident-risk, battle-repair and negotiation formulas, with normal caps. The current shared budget gives signing fees of 520 / 1,040 / 2,080 cr and daily wages of 12 / 24 / 48 cr for Specialist / Veteran / Elite respectively. Tier benefits are +1 / +2 / +3 effective skill. Legacy hires keep their original contract and +1 skill benefit. All retained members receive wages, including off-duty days. Transit wages are included and paid once in the departure quote. Stopped service and additional delay days invoice wages once; any cash shortfall uses the existing original-charter balance. Release at a station stops future wages without refunding the signing fee. Six possible monthly identities are divided randomly into three ten-day travel windows. Each person can make one brief visit, lasting three to seven days; at most two potential visitors belong to a window, and a probability draw determines whether each visits. It is normal to find no recruits. The original candidate identities and all signed agreements retain their generation rules; availability now depends on the encounter model below. These specialists provide operational benefits instead of an effective-skill bonus. Costs use the same tiered work budget. New matching crew and relic abilities use the strongest source; captain background and disposition modifiers remain additive, with normal risk, accuracy, heat and repair caps. Benefits stop when stood down, released, stowed or sold. Travel safety does not alter physical travel time or propulsion requirements.

Finding travelling recruits

Combat, liberation, rescue, passenger work, clinic work, repairs, trading and exploration favour related professions. Owned, previously purchased, sold and recorded lost classes contribute to ship history; classes discovered in a saved encounter register remain recorded after sale or loss. Only earned levels contribute, so relics and crew cannot manufacture recruitment progress. The first enquiry freezes the career context for that station and ten-day window. Later achievements affect future windows, while reopening, reloading, changing duties or returning to the same commons cannot reroll the frozen visit. Independent stable hashes choose the visitor order, arrival, duration and probability draw; they never consume combat or travel RNG. Appearance probabilities describe scheduled visits, not daily hiring success. Visitors can be absent before arrival, leave on the displayed date, and refuse to re-sign after release during the same monthly identity cycle. Hiring before departure uses the existing finite crew slots, signing fees and recurring wages. Existing hires remain aboard after their original visit ends.

Operational support

Fatigue and incident modifiers reduce the corresponding base amount multiplicatively. Accuracy is added before the normal hit-chance cap; guard protection reduces the guarded hull-damage multiplier subject to its floor. Cooling subtracts heat from laser shots; repair adds restored integrity, capped at full hull. Relic powers and every crew specialty are included in the generic formula data.

13 / Station liberation

Blockade liberation commissions

Distance and reports

The chance is a new outbreak draw per station per 30-day period, not a daily or per-journey roll. Earth-group stations use the protected local baseline; orbital phase sets other distances. A new outbreak persists until defeated, independently of mission acceptance. The world-start date is saved on the first successful action after this mechanic is installed (day zero for new captains), so older voyages do not inherit years of previously unmodelled outbreaks. Each station’s stable draws are independent of travel/combat randomness. Refreshes, forecasts, reloads and accepting or declining a commission cannot reroll them.

The two nearest eligible licensed destinations are checked for station blockades. An authority may have no commission; accepting one secures the existing quoted payment. Travelling to a blockaded station immediately launches the same actual tactical battle, accepted commission or not, before docking, customs, delivery handover or station services. Victory must defeat the entire hostile formation. An accepted or withdrawn commission for that target is cleared by this battle and pays its original reward once at the issuer; an uncommissioned encounter has no invented authority payment or combat bounty. Every station can be liberated once per voyage. Unrelated battles, retreats, surrender and dispersal do not count.

After a failed attempt the fleet remains outside the closed docks. Retry the blockade or explicitly contract recovery to the departure station. Recovery uses the actual fleet’s return-route propellant, provisions, launch-window cost, 900 cr per operational hull and physical route days; it is paid once from cash with ordinary credit fallback. Existing combat damage, lost hulls and cargo, emergency stabilization, crew wages, passage time, interest, berths and deadlines continue. It does not grant access to the blockaded exchange.

Post-liberation exchange recovery

The premium is local, conditional and strongest for net consumers, fading over 30 days. It applies only to ordinary exchange goods, including owned warehouse stock sold through the exchange. Contraband, relics, ship sales, freight/mission payments and supplier prices retain their existing rules. The recovery sell quote stays below the matching marginal ask, preventing a local buy/resell profit or XP loop as reserves change. Finite market stock, owned cargo, client custody and purchase basis remain enforced. The ordinary baseline cache is kept separate from player recovery quotes. The generic dashboard retains its approved unconditional daily-cycle figures and distributions; these conditional voyage outcomes are described here rather than mixed into those statistics.

14 / Reputation

Reputation and station reports

These replace flat positive score awards; their original weights still distinguish deliveries (3), first clinic aid (5), victories (4), repeat clinic supply (2) and traffic/research reports (1), before captain modifiers. Negative awards apply immediately at the existing magnitude and erase partial progress. Scores remain integers from −100 to 100; existing earned scores/history are preserved. Wider reputation still contributes 25% to effective local reception; public/local customs trust remains 75%/25%. Service discounts, bank review and introductions use their existing bounded standing rules. Ordinary daily exchange prices do not change.

A canteen/commons reputation conversation can spread a recorded public deed or substantial local service/misconduct from another station. Reports expire after 180 days; an account reaches at most three other stations once each, and each station considers one account per 30 days. Same-day accounts of the same sign from the same origin share one dissemination limit. Positive reports contribute base 1 public and base 2 local progress; negative reports cost up to 1 public and 2 local points immediately. Receipts persist across reloads and contacts. Gossip never becomes a new source, and praise dated on or before newer misconduct cannot mask it. No new account means no score change. Drinks, browsing and refreshing never grant reputation.

Trading work, credit and scheduled hulls

One persistent work record pools known sale margins and recorded operating costs across ships, warehouses, split orders and reloads. Departure settles the preceding work; stationary trading settles on its next sale after 30 days. A profitable result earns up to 20 Commerce XP, with no new allowance per click. Client work retains its own existing rewards.

New-charter emergency headroom is 3,000 cr above the financed starter-hull balance. Expansion seasoning is clamp((profitable voyages + recorded legacy voyages − 80) / 40, 0, 1). Earned net operating income, eligible repayments and recoverable assets support expansion only in proportion to seasoning. The asset ceiling and local branch ceiling still apply; borrowed cash cannot support itself. Conservative shared batch liquidation replaces first-unit bid multiplication.

Basic Swift, Kestrel, Heron and Peregrine batches are guaranteed but finite at qualified suppliers. One sold-out basic hull may be reserved for the next class batch, with no deposit and full catalogue price due at collection. Specialist scarcity remains.

Finite blockades last 21–45 days in 180-day station slots. Reports, shipping and arrival encounters share a daily episode schedule, with first-year Earth corridor protection and post-victory cooldown. Accepted authority payments still require player victory and can never be claimed twice.

14 / Credit

Daily rate momentum, fixed loans and trading demand

Public wholesale conditions are sampled each ship-day. These examples are synthetic and never read a player’s voyage. A daily economic target changes acceleration and velocity; it does not overwrite the rate.

Production and consumption are the exchange’s seasonal physical flows; each listed good receives one normalized vote. Export working-capital demand uses the best positive gross consumer bid versus exporter ask, excluding restricted cargo, private discounts, freight fees and voyage costs. Station risk baselines, size and resupply lead settings remain fixed profiles; the goods signals and funding conditions are sampled daily. A stronger upward rate velocity widens the deposit/loan spread proportionally, while a stronger downward velocity narrows it. The cross-branch ceiling can further reduce a deposit quote to preserve the no-instant-carry rule. Small changes can round to the same displayed rate.

Each quote is reconstructed from at most 240 preceding daily targets, initialized at the first target with zero velocity. The bounded warm-up avoids dependence on which date or branch was opened first; no growing rate history is saved. Only public seasonal summaries, a bounded target cache and eight completed quotes are cached. Opening pages, player balances, trade perturbations, reputation and refresh do not reroll public rates. Reputation still affects borrowing limits.

A fixed loan locks its own annual quote for that draw; later draws use current terms and never enlarge an old cheap agreement. The station-specific 1.5–2.5-point annual locking premium remains separate from the momentum-sensitive deposit/loan spread. There is no upfront points charge or early repayment fee. Original fixed contracts and the 4% charter retain their signed terms. Floating loans and deposits use every elapsed daily quote, including during long voyages, without changing already recorded balances or elapsed history. Repayments settle highest current rates first.

Every current floating loan exceeds every current deposit yield, preventing an immediate funded-deposit loop. Older fixed borrowing can still cost less than a later deposit yield. Net carry must account for interest paid during travel, fuel, provisions, berths and other liabilities. Deposits remain branch-local and cash in transit earns no deposit interest. Gross goods margins are funding-demand signals, not promised returns.
