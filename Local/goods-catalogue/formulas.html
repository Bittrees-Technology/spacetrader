01 / Exchange

From base value to a quoted price

Storage fill sets scarcity; geography adds a modest logistics adjustment and the supply tier sets the spread. The public baseline repeats for the same item, station and cycle day; actual trade quotes also depend on stock. All tables here use generic reference examples.

logistics = 1 + .018 × tanh(ln(G)) + .01 × tanh(ln(1 + AU) / 3)
referenceMid = max(5, round(base × logistics))
capacity = the physical station/product storage ceiling
centerStock = capacity / 2
fill(q) = clamp(q / capacity, 0, 1)
weight(q) = [1 + tanh(2 × (1 − 2 × fill(q))) / tanh(2)] / 2
lowFactor = exp(−.085); highFactor = exp(.085)
mid(q) = max(5, round(referenceMid ×
         [lowFactor + (highFactor − lowFactor) × weight(q)]))
50%-full price = referenceMid × (lowFactor + highFactor) / 2, before rounding
ask = ceil(mid(q − .5) × (1 + spread))
bid = max(1, floor(mid(q + .5) × (1 − spread)))
order total = sum of each successive lot’s marginal quote

Textiles, refined alloy, life-support filters and magnesium use an acyclic manufacturing floor. The reference value is at least ceil((sum(recipe input × generic local ask) + normal processing services + 1) / (1 − spread) × exp(impact × 0.5 / minimumDepth)). This retains the manufacturing reference and its conservative rounding allowance. The actual output bid still follows stock fill; this floor does not promise profitable processing at every fill level. It uses public dated baseline quotes, without a captain’s stock changes or private premiums. Reversible essential utility chemistry instead receives a station appropriation equal to any actual input-and-service shortfall after all coproduct receipts; the payment cannot be claimed by a captain.

G is the location diagnostic used by the modest logistics overlay. Seasonal stock changes move along the same centered curve; the seasonal and reserve diagnostics are not added again as price multipliers. Whole-credit rounding can make cheaper goods appear stepped.

Buying reduces local reserves and raises subsequent asks; selling increases reserves and lowers subsequent bids. Every lot clears against the marginal stock curve below, so order totals include price movement. The seasonal baseline and spread tier remain public and repeatable; actual voyage quotes also reflect its saved station stock. Earned XP, reputation and the dealer-activity label do not independently multiply ordinary exchange prices.

03 / Value ladder

Commodity base values

base = round(80 × 1.25^tier / 5) × 5

Tiers begin at zero. Every step increases the unrounded value by 25%; the final base rounds to the nearest 5 credits. Restricted goods and the new raw-resource families use deliberately assigned fractional tiers; existing commodity IDs keep their original tiers.

Stock scale is separate from capital value. An explicit inventoryScale wins; otherwise the original, pre-ladder base gives 0.08 at ≥2,000 cr, 0.14 at ≥900, 0.25 at ≥600, then 0.4 for special-handling goods, otherwise 1. The first matching rule applies.

04 / Geography

Regional and local factors

localWeight = 0.16 + 0.12 × min(1, sqrt(orbitalRadiusKm / 1,000,000))
G = R^0.8 × (L / R)^localWeight

For the original six staples, R is the geometric mean of station item-factors in the same planet system: exp(mean(ln(factor))). L is the selected station’s factor. A missing factor is 1.

For added commodities carrying a proxy field, the system’s exporter status and distance from the Sun determine R; L equals R:

R = 0.90 + ln(1 + AU) × 0.025    if any station in the system exports it
R = 1.04 + ln(1 + AU) × 0.055    otherwise
L = R

AU is the station’s configured system distance in astronomical units. Orbital radius is its local orbital radius in kilometres.

05 / Local economy

Production, consumption and seasonal reserves

stationSells = production > 0 OR declared retail import OR transit stock
stationBuys = consumption > 0 OR stationSells
player Buy = buy from station, subject to stock and access
player Sell = sell owned cargo to station, subject to buyer and ownership

Gross production and gross consumption determine trade direction; net surplus is not an eligibility gate. A station can manufacture a good and still consume more than it produces. A factory may buy an ingredient without offering it back as retail merchandise. The same policy feeds the live exchange, visited commodity directory and generic catalogue. Buy-price columns describe player purchases; sell-price columns describe player sale proceeds.

Each station has its own size, delivery lead time, exports, inputs, imports and transit goods. Seasons represent procurement and production schedules, including at airless stations.

process capacity x[j] = reviewed mean main-output batches/day
B[g,j] = output and coproduct coefficients; A[g,j] = ingredient coefficients
P₀[station,g] = sum(B[g,j] × x[j]) for local processes
factoryInputs₀[station,g] = sum(A[g,j] × x[j])
finalDemand[g] = sum((B[g,j] − A[g,j]) × x[j]) across the network
C₀[station,g] = factoryInputs₀[station,g] + reviewed local share × finalDemand[g]
sum(P₀[g]) = sum(C₀[g]) for each good
primary inputs = final use + unrecovered process waste, at full-cycle balance

The reviewed 348-process capacity solution uses complete mass recipes and explicit alternate recovery routes. Coefficients are physical 250 kg lots, independent of value tier and inventory scaling. Intermediate mean output is 10,000 lots/day and higher-tier mean output is 3,000, supported by 20,000 mean primary collection lots. Gross repeated-stage output is 33,000, not 33,000 newly extracted lots. Ores and brines have no artificial household final-use sink. Each station has eight unrestricted core goods with at least five mean net-surplus lots/day under full supply; real inventories and inputs still limit sales and output.

Whole-day integrals use peaks at 600.5 and 720.5 in continuous time. All process capacity schedules use a common phase; the old sector-offset calendar is no longer active. Final demand retains the 120-day lag, without forcing factory inputs onto an unrelated wave. The 12,000–28,000, 6,000–14,000 and 1,800–4,200 limits are planned full-supply outputs. Actual disrupted production may fall below them. Every ingredient is reserved from opening stock plus arrivals; progressive filling redistributes shared inputs when a competing line is blocked elsewhere. Newly produced goods cannot feed another plant until the following daily tick. Outputs and useful coproducts are credited once, with waste recorded separately. Electrolysis oxygen is now real saleable coproduct stock, not a second free oxygen batch. Household final use and client-delivery credits are settled only once. See all process recipes.

06 / Inventory

Stock, bonding curves and freight

quantity[d+1] = quantity[d] + actualProduction − actualUse
                + receivedCargo − dispatchedCargo
nominal route = matching producer net surplus → consumer shortfall
route capacity = 2 × planned mean daily shipment
route lead = ceil(destination lead × 0.25) in the same system,
             otherwise destination lead (at least 1 day)
extra request = min(source excess / route lead, destination shortfall / route lead)
dispatch = min(real source stock, nominal + extra request, route capacity)
protected = 3 days of factory inputs + 2 days of final use
retail allocation = max(8, floor(2 × (planned daily production + mean net imports)))
available = min(floor(quantity − protected), retail allocation − already bought today)
buy n: quantity − n; sell n: quantity + n

Every received shipment has a matching earlier source withdrawal. Freight stays in transit for the full route delay. A verified blockade suspends departures and holds arriving goods in transit until the route reopens; an unconfirmed statistical report does not destroy cargo. Missing inputs curtail factory output; households cannot consume absent goods. Ordinary routes have no invented random losses. Restricted dealers, player cargo losses and discarded process by-products remain separate, explicit channels.

Fleet trades, warehouse trades and ordinary client pickups use the same physical station/product pool. There is no independent reserve reset or unlimited restock. Surplus production continues to accumulate if routes close; sales are rejected at the storage safety limit rather than silently deleting overflow. Earlier v43, v44, v45, v47, v48 and v50 v2 market books retain their exact physical stock and freight on upgrade, including compact pipelines and shipments on closed routes. New capacity affects subsequent production, never owned cargo or cash. New commodity opening reserves are recorded separately as introduced stock; they do not refill the existing32goods or invent an old shipment. Only market deviations and losslessly compressed pending shipments are saved; R1 runs and the v51 D1 dictionary preserve exact Float64 quantities, with bounded station, good and route counts. Reading the board never advances time. Existing v1 exchange receipts retain their old evolution through migration day; owned cargo, basis, contracts and money are preserved.

The S-curve is centered at half of each station/product’s storage capacity. Equal stock fractions either side of 50% have equal and opposite deviations from the center price before rounding and spread. Empty storage reaches the upper reference limit, and full storage reaches the lower limit. Operating reserves still guide procurement and commercial needs, independently of this price midpoint. Seasonal production, use and shipments move the physical stock along this one curve; no seasonal price shock is added on top.

Explore the full centered stock-price curve. The chart runs from 0 to Max with a dashed 50% midpoint. Quantities are 250 kg net lots; ship holds, packaging and manufacturing ratios retain their physical meaning. Each lot clears at its own marginal half-lot price, so split orders settle identically and reversing a purchase crosses the spread. Liberation premiums remain capped below the ask at the matching sale midpoint. Real shipments can close price gaps; later production, use and replenishment can reopen them. Route expenses determine actual profit.

Physical station storage

Each station/product has a stable storage ceiling: max(100 lots, ceil(1.25 × highest planned seasonal inventory)). Production tapers above 90% occupancy including committed inbound shipments, and every batch must fit all coproducts before consuming ingredients. Incoming cargo reserves room, full stores defer unloading, and new dispatch cannot exceed uncommitted receiving space. Exchange sales, Max and Sell all respect physical room and the remaining daily buying allowance. The allowance is two days of mean consumption plus one day of net export production, multiplied by inventory scale and a coverage factor clamped from .05 to1.5. Signed procurement reserves its receiving quantities. Buying and selling cannot reset the same-day counters. These finite commercial budgets do not refill or remove physical goods. Existing excess is retained and worked down through use and outgoing trade.

Ordinary client freight

Acceptance removes each product from available source stock, loads compatible active holds, and freezes terms. Clients consume deliveries: destination exchange stock is unchanged at handover. Delivered lots offset subsequent local final-use demand, so the receiving client is not counted twice; this does not offset industrial recipe inputs. Client cargo cannot be sold, donated or counted as collateral. Late, lost or abandoned freight follows the signed restitution and breach terms. Surviving cargo stays sealed client property with no owned purchase basis. Only explicit theft converts retained cargo into owned goods after full restitution and the breach fee are billed.

Total active capacity must support the tier. Actual free compatible holds and real source stock are rechecked atomically at acceptance. The board matches actual source surplus above local reserves to receiving shortages and expected use, less inbound consignments and unused demand credits. It offers up to six distinct destinations, one shipment each. Active same-origin routes remain reserved across refreshes; each route/cycle permits one booking. Freight commission comes from station margin after the quoted operating budget. The source bid and destination ask are internal acquisition/invoice references, distinct from a player’s source-ask/destination-bid arbitrage. Risk, receiving urgency, duration, product handling and existing support affect the share within its cap. Expenses accrue from actual departures and are shared across carried cargo, preventing duplicate reimbursement. Capital and a percentage of cargo replacement value are not paid as expenses.

source cost = sum(source bid total for every product)
receiving invoice = sum(destination ask total for every product)
expenses ceiling = loaded reference hull operating costs
pool = max(0, receiving invoice - source cost - expenses ceiling)
share = min(0.25, (0.08 + 0.10*risk + 0.04*urgency
                   + 0.02*min(1,routeDays/180) + 0.01*(products-1)/3) * support * captain)
commission = floor(pool * share / (1 + share))
station retained profit = pool - commission
commission <= floor(station retained profit * 0.25)
payment = commission + min(expenses ceiling, recorded allocated voyage expenses)
missing restitution = sum(ceil(locked product replacement value * units lost / original units))
ordinary breach = missing restitution + signed breach fee
new breach fee = max(100, ceil(station retained profit * 0.10))
theft = full locked replacement value + breach fee; available funds then charter debt

Freight and navigation share actual voyage risk = 1 − (1 − corridor chance) × (1 − approach chance), capped at 0.55. Origin, flight duration, belt and regional exposure affect the corridor; destination exposure affects the approach. Confirmed destination blockades have pricing risk 1 and force an encounter before docking. Thirty-day public traffic samples adjust actual forecasts: deviation = clamp((reports − expected reports)/(samples + 30), −0.04, 0.04), fading linearly over 120 days from issue to projected arrival. Public circulars describe station approaches without personal creditor or loadout effects; your voyage may also include confirmed intelligence and creditor exposure. Both raids, one raid or no raid can occur. A corridor contact saves and interrupts the remaining flight before arrival. Accepted freight terms never reprice.

Unaccepted freight deadlines stay fixed to the dispatch issue date plus the loaded reference flight and clearance and 90 days. The board hides an offer once the current loaded fleet would arrive after that deadline; acceptance rechecks before stock changes. Delivery on the due date is allowed. Signed jobs expire once the calendar passes the deadline, including elapsed transit and incident delays.

Warehouse tenant leases

Canteen introductions offer fixed 14-, 30- or 60-day terms. Tenant goods occupy real space but remain outside owned inventory, collateral and net worth. Capacity, goods, terms and the offer’s 30-day cycle determine availability; no player save is used by this reference.

10 / Catalogue

Baseline cycle lows, highs and local balance

These generic daily snapshots describe undisturbed public seasonal reserves. Player shipments and local liberation premiums can move actual voyage quotes outside this baseline range. No active voyage, private inventory or trading strategy is imported into the reference distributions.

Maximum gain, loss and quote frequency

max gain = max(0, highest sell − lowest buy)
max loss = min(0, lowest sell − highest buy)
gain % = max gain / lowest buy × 100
loss % = max loss / highest buy × 100

P(max gain quote pair) = count(lowest-buy exchange quotes) / count(all buy quotes)
                      × count(highest-sell exchange quotes) / count(all sell quotes)
P(max loss quote pair) = count(highest-buy exchange quotes) / count(all buy quotes)
                      × count(lowest-sell exchange quotes) / count(all sell quotes)

Amounts are per traded unit and exclude fuel, provisions, fees, repairs and losses in transit. Percentages use the purchase price for that outcome. If all price pairs lose money, maximum gain is 0; if all pairs profit, maximum loss is 0. Missing buy or sell prices give N/A.

Quote frequency assumes two independent, uniform selections from the matching exchange station/day observations across the complete 1,440-day phase cycle. Each station/day counts once; buy observations include only stations that list the item, while sell observations include all accepting exchanges. This measures exact quoted extrema, not the probability of making a profitable journey. Actual journeys have ordered dates, travel costs, access and finite stock.

Typical gains, losses and their probabilities

The Return columns selector changes the leading value and sorting basis for all four return columns. Choose Credits first or Return % first, then click a gain/loss heading to sort. Switching basis preserves the selected column and direction. Both values remain visible and in CSV exports.

pair margin = sell − buy
pair return % = (sell − buy) / buy × 100
N = listed-exchange buy observations × accepting-exchange sell observations
P(gain) = number of pairs with margin > 0 / N
P(loss) = number of pairs with margin < 0 / N
P(break even) = number of pairs with margin = 0 / N
Avg gain = sum(positive pair margins) / number of winning pairs
Avg loss = sum(negative pair margins) / number of losing pairs
Average % = mean of each winning / losing pair’s return percentage
Expected margin = P(gain) × Avg gain + P(loss) × Avg loss

The average columns describe ordinary winning and losing quote pairs, using all daily price observations rather than exceptional top/bottom 10% tails. Each station/day counts once across the complete repeating cycle. Frequently quoted prices receive proportionally more weight. The displayed percentage is the average individual return on purchase price, not a return calculated from mean prices.

The frequency below each average is its share of all quote pairs, including break-even pairs in the denominator. Gain, loss and break-even probabilities sum to 100%. If a population contains no winners or no losers, that conditional average is N/A with 0% of pairs. Unsupported markets and missing buy/sell populations show N/A without a probability.

These are exact results for the stated model of two independent, uniformly selected exchange station/day observations. They are not a forecast for a particular route, ship or trading strategy. Departures, travel times, stock, licences and running costs are excluded. Traveller and restricted-dealer availability has no defined weight here, so those channels are excluded. Weighted histograms and cumulative sums calculate the results without allocating billions of quote pairs.

Daily buy and sell price distributions

The final column pools one daily quote per station across every day of the 1,440-day pricing cycle, separately for purchases and sales. Buy quotes include stations listing that good; sell quotes include all accepting exchanges. Each available station-day has equal weight. Matching market-access and item filters determine which exchange rows contribute. Histograms retain the exact count at every quoted price, so a price quoted on 100 days contributes 100 observations.

Buy population = { ask(station, day) for every listed station, day = 0…1,439 }
Sell population = { bid(station, day) for every accepting station, day = 0…1,439 }
N = number of station-day observations on that side
mean price = Σ(price × count) / N
population variance = Σ(count × (price − mean)²) / N
standard deviation = sqrt(population variance)
median = middle price, or average of the two middle prices when N is even
bin frequency = observations in that price bin / N
at/below = count(quote ≤ marker price) / N
at/above = count(quote ≥ marker price) / N

The solid cyan curve is Buy; the dashed gold curve is Sell. Both use the same horizontal credit-price scale and vertical frequency scale within that good. The Return columns selector affects gain/loss columns only; price curves always use credits. Each side is normalized to its own quote count. A sell-only selection still has a sell curve, with no fabricated buy observations. Intermittent travellers, restricted dealers and equipment offers have no daily availability distribution and contribute no invented observations.

Hover, focus or click markers for the exact minimum, maximum, mean, median and ±1, ±2 and ±3 population standard deviations, plus the share of daily quotes at or below/above that price. The two shares can overlap at tied prices. A low buy percentile helps identify an unusually cheap purchase quote; a high sell percentile identifies an unusually high bid. These frequencies describe public prices, not transaction volume or the probability of making a trade.

The drawing uses 128 common price bins, light mass-conserving local smoothing and shape-preserving cubic interpolation. It is an empirical price-frequency curve, not a fitted bell curve. Means, medians, extrema and deviations use the unsmoothed integer quotes. No density extends beyond a side’s observed minimum or maximum. Deviation markers beyond quoted support are labeled outside the quoted range; negative theoretical markers sit at the zero-price edge. Axis limits may extend beyond the observed quotes to accommodate those markers.

Holding goods allows you to choose when to sell. These curves show how frequently prices occur over a full cycle; they do not preserve timing order or estimate the wait until a target price returns. Travel, stock, access, storage, bank interest and other carrying costs still affect the result.
