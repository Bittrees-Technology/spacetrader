Getting started

Your first run

  1. Create your captain and compare the ten starting exchange goods.
  2. Find a buyer, check the loaded voyage budget and keep working cash for fuel and setbacks.
  3. Buy your cargo, travel and sell within the receiving station’s allowance.

Freight is optional client work. Review its payment, cargo ownership and due date before accepting. Tasks lists current commitments; the Journal records what happened.

Travel & stations

Station locations

Locations read as station · local orbit or moon · parent world. Stickney Dock · Phobos · Mars and Swift Anchorage · Deimos · Mars belong to the same Mars system. Meridian Station · Orbit · Earth, Kepler Shipyard · High orbit · Earth and Shackleton Relay · Moon · Earth belong to the Earth system. This format appears on destinations, tasks, deliveries, assignments, ship locations and station records.

Powered travel

Ships accelerate for half the flight, turn around and brake for the second half. Flight time is estimated as 2√(distance / acceleration). Circular planetary phases lead the destination at arrival; paths detour outside a 0.25 AU solar exclusion. Local legs follow a conservative corridor around the parent. A one-day interplanetary clearance replaces the old launch-window wait. Cargo, equipment, propellant and the slowest escort affect acceleration. The map follows the ship’s calendar and the planned arrival time.

Establishments

Exchange

Cargo brokers match arriving shipments with the needs of local habitats and visiting crews. Consignment records, supply certificates and freight manifests move across the counter while handlers prepare sealed trade lots for transfer. The exchange deals in processed goods, linking this station’s everyday needs to the wider shipping network.

Freight office

Dispatchers coordinate client-owned shipments between licensed destinations, pairing available hold space with delivery commitments. Each consignment has a named destination, a timetable and handling terms to review before acceptance. Incoming notices and delivery receipts trace the movement of goods between otherwise distant communities.

Dry dock

Drive engineers and hull technicians keep visiting vessels ready for their next passage. Service crews work through propellant loading, integrity repairs and engineering stores, with specialist ammunition handled through the dock’s own supply catalogue. Maintenance here follows the condition of each hull and the equipment actually fitted aboard it.

Fleet registry

The registry brings local ship offers, outfitting records and fleet administration together at the spaceport. Its staff reconcile hull ownership, fitting specifications and berth assignments as captains plan their next purchase or refit. Available vessels depend on local stock; the registry’s records also help crews coordinate ships already in their fleet.

Patrol office

Patrol staff issue capture warrants, register the bounties you accept and share the latest intelligence from surrounding routes. A report points to possible trouble during ordinary travel; the office’s work is to connect observations, verify outcomes and arrange the handover of captured raiders.

Communications

Station coordinators arrange ship and fleet escort contracts, passenger charters, medical transfers and evacuation flotillas, alongside arrivals, departures and navigation reports. Route briefings, cargo manifests and delivery timetables share desk space with observations submitted by returning crews. The office helps captains prepare a departure around current access, crew readiness and the practical hazards of the surrounding corridors.

Clinic

Medical coordinators oversee recovery berths, treatment schedules and the clinic’s reserve supplies. Visiting crews can review care, contribute eligible medical goods or discuss supervised research when work is available. The clinic balances the needs of transient crews with a resident community that depends on reliable shipments and carefully maintained equipment.

Canteen / commons

The commons gives station workers and visiting crews a place to eat, rest and exchange news between shifts. Conversations move from ordinary work to route rumours, personal histories and occasional introductions. Familiar faces share the room with travellers passing through; what reaches a captain’s table depends on who is present and willing to talk.

Credit cooperative

The credit cooperative maintains this station’s deposit accounts and lending desk. Its manager reviews local terms, available borrowing and repayment records with visiting captains, while the wider financial record keeps track of obligations elsewhere. The counter is a meeting point between long shipping schedules and the everyday credit needs of the station.

Port authority

The Port Authority combines the local clearance desk with an interplanetary government liaison. Its officer connects residents and visiting crews with their governing council, explains the Compact and the Veyr and Ilyri treaty records, and identifies the authority responsible for cross-border questions. Access applications still follow the destinations, experience and vessel qualifications handled by this office; the archive tells how those shared obligations were negotiated.

Warehouse

The warehouse provides permanent local storage for eligible owned cargo. Custodians reconcile bay ownership, delivery receipts and collection manifests so goods remain accounted for while a fleet is away. Stored merchandise stays at this station until collected; the facility keeps those local holdings distinct from cargo aboard travelling ships.

Establishment controls

Each establishment keeps its character conversation beside the relevant services. The Dry dock selector applies to every quoted service and its actual target. Changing ships clears a partial fuel amount. Communications uses the same selected destination for readiness, route plotting and briefing. Its section buttons jump to transport, departure or survey work when more than one is available.

The Fleet registry keeps the selected ship, price and purchase or sale action visible while you inspect the hull. Upgrade rows show their benefits before fitting. Review a diplomatic mandate to read its full route and terms before accepting; return to the authority to continue. Patrol keeps the local watch above bounties, with Read full report opening the complete dated circular. Its Known routes badge describes the highest assessed risk on your known network, not just the current station.

Commons accounts remain available in the dated notebook after buying a round. Select a headline to read its complete account and plot a reported location. A round is limited to one per station per ship-day, and useful news is not guaranteed. A seven-day layover costs 14 cr in provisions; deadlines, bank interest and berth fees continue.

Clinic recovery restores up to 40 readiness over two days; full treatment restores 100 over five. These options appear when the crew needs care. Before ending client freight, review the restitution, breach charge, cargo outcome and suspension. Available funds pay breach charges first; unpaid amounts become charter debt at Meridian. Stealing carries full restitution and the longer suspension. Surviving ordinary-breach goods remain client-owned until returned. Original purchase contracts retain their original rules.

Navigation learning and repeated routes

Departure learning reflects route complexity and actual work rather than a large flat launch award. The raw award starts at three for a local leg or eight between planetary systems, adds up to ten for the logarithm of physical flight days, up to four for assessed hazards, five for a first destination, and three if new useful deliveries, aid, victories or profitable trading voyages were recorded since the last departure award. Waiting alone adds nothing. Existing captain XP bonuses apply afterwards.

The same corridor, in either direction, earns 100%, 65%, 35%, then 15% of that raw award when repeated within ninety game days, rounded down with a one-XP minimum. The learning window and route register are bounded to sixty-four corridors. Normal local work remains useful; repeating one safe leg cannot stand in for experience of every profession.

Twenty percent of voyage learning after corridor repetition and before captain XP bonuses accumulates as reportable observations, capped at twenty-five. A one-day traffic report pays its whole-XP balance once and clears that balance. Repeated observations with no whole XP add no reputation. This report cannot restore the old flat five-XP-per-launch advantage. Current XP totals remain earned records; relic and crew effective-skill bonuses do not replace earned qualifications.

Continuing memories store scene IDs, choices, outcomes, dates and compact counters. They do not duplicate every transcript. New personal, regional, crew, correspondence and mastery actions appear in the Journal and durable action history.

Planning route fuel

Load route fuel solves the planned flight again as propellant mass changes. It loads enough for the journey and an arrival maneuver reserve, while keeping fuel already aboard. The reserve covers a 0.25 km/s avoidance maneuver and a 0.15 km/s withdrawal, with a 0.15 tonne minimum. Larger and heavier fleets therefore retain more propellant. If the journey fits the tanks but the full reserve does not, loading fills the tanks and navigation warns that the reserve is limited.

The route readout shows expected fleet burn and fuel left with the current load; loading fuel updates flight time, cost and arrival estimates. Commanding a formation maneuver or withdrawing burns fuel for every moving ship. Individual live-battle positioning burns fuel for the ship making that move. Berthed ships are excluded from travelling fuel, cargo and maneuver costs. Hulls, cargo, installed fittings, ammunition, passengers and mission loads affect propulsion mass; drive fittings improve specific impulse.

Propellant and people

Asterion drives provide 50,000–56,000 s base specific impulse, combining high-thrust fusion propulsion with advanced heat management. The rocket equation includes acceleration, braking and a conservative orbital maneuver allowance. Fuel remains mass that you carry and buy; hydrogen reaction mass and fusion pellets are one certified load. Life-support mass, detailed radiation dose, fuel production and biological aging are not simulated. Calendar time and loan interest are.

Raids during a voyage

Navigation and freight use the same voyage forecast. Corridor interception and destination-approach challenges are separate chances: either, both or neither can occur. For chances r and a, the chance of at least one raid is 1 − (1 − r)(1 − a). Ordinary combined risk is capped at 55%. A confirmed destination blockade is a separate forced encounter before docking, even without accepting a liberation commission. A high-risk quote does not guarantee a raid.

The destination approach depends on its distance from Earth, local exposure and dated watch. The corridor also depends on origin, flight duration, belt exposure and regional raider history. Personal creditor exposure and confirmed intelligence may affect your own voyage. Public circulars describe station approaches, independently of your ship or finances. Their thirty-day background accounts adjust the actual forecast, with influence fading over 120 days on longer flights. Changing origin or travel date can therefore change a quote for the same destination. Accepted freight keeps its dated fee and assessment.

A corridor raid visibly interrupts playback before arrival and saves the elapsed calendar and remaining flight. Resolve it before continuing; station services, docking, discovery and inspections await actual arrival. Planned propellant and travel provisions, including named-crew transit wages, are committed once at departure. Avoidance or combat can add delays and extra costs. A later approach raid can still occur. Skipping animation stops at the contact, and reloading the recorded stage does not reroll it.

Regional access

Visit port authorities as you travel to discover the licences each one offers. Some require navigation experience, a suitable ship, or both. Build navigation experience by completing voyages or studying routes with station contacts. A qualifying ship must be your flagship or an assigned escort present at the station, with at least 35% integrity.

Licence prices reflect your career discount and destinations already covered. Fees are paid from available cash. Licences are permanent and non-refundable, and previously earned access remains valid. Access does not replace route fuel, hull integrity or escort readiness requirements. A disabled purchase button means a qualification, encounter or available-funds check is unmet; inspect the licence below for its requirements. The quoted price already includes your career discount and any destinations you already have access to.

Ships & fleet

Fleet overview & command

The active and berthed registers list ships from lowest to highest full catalogue value, including installed fittings and specializations. The flagship stays first. Values are replacement costs, rather than discounted sale offers; hull condition, fuel, stores and cargo do not change this ordering. Berthed ships keep their station beside their name.

Select another local ship and choose Make flagship to transfer command. Both hulls keep their cargo, fuel, equipment and assignments, and the previous flagship remains in the active fleet as an escort. Remote ships require a rendezvous first; embarked or towed ships must be deployed or detached, and a replacement needs at least 15% integrity. Command changes are unavailable during an encounter or battle.

Fleet registry: ships, upgrades & sales

Use Purchase a ship to inspect new hulls and ships sold at this station. Ship upgrades outfits an owned local vessel. Purchases keep your flagship and berth here until you assign an escort or take command.

Sell a ship pays 75% of catalogue hull value multiplied by its integrity, plus 50% of installed upgrade costs, including any duty specialization. Fuel, spares and ammunition stay aboard without adding to the bid. Clear cargo, passengers, mission assignments, embarked craft and tows first. Take command of another local ship before selling your flagship.

The sold hull remains at that station indefinitely with its condition, equipment and stores. Repurchasing costs the full catalogue hull price plus every installed upgrade and specialization at full fitting cost. Stock is one physical ship, and purchases still require class experience and fleet space. Ordinary new-hull deliveries are separate.

Ship upgrade reference

Armor reinforcement

Reinforced hull plating reduces the remaining unarmored damage by 7% per tier. Base armor differs by class; shield absorption occurs first.

Engine optimization

Adds 2.5% specific impulse and 1% base dry mass per tier.

Propellant tanks

Adds 15% tank capacity and 2% base dry mass per tier.

Cargo framework

Adds 10% gross hold capacity and 3% base dry mass per tier. Internal refit space is deducted afterward.

Fire-control sensors

Adds 5 percentage points to weapon accuracy per tier; normal accuracy limits still apply.

Energy weapon package

Class-specific pulse, beam, burst or tracking lasers. Higher tiers improve total output and may add emitters; see the fitted weapon table. Energy based: no ammunition or cargo-space charge.

Kinetic weapon package

Class-specific coilguns, railguns or rapid-fire cannons. Combat hulls include a factory battery; civilian hulls add one at tier 1.

Shield generator

Factory shields vary by class. Retrofitting adds 1× / 2.2× / 3.6× the class generator rating. Restores 10% capacity per round after two rounds without a hit.

Missile / torpedo package

Guided missiles or heavy torpedoes by class. Higher tiers improve warhead strength and may add launch tubes. Ammunition and reload time apply.

Dry dock services

One selector applies to fuel, hull repairs, engineering spares and ammunition. Active fleet is selected by default and includes the flagship and travelling escorts. Select an individual active ship or all active ships. When you own berthed ships at this station, a separate group offers each berthed ship, all berthed ships here, or all ships here together. Ships berthed at other stations never appear. Remote ships must arrive before they can be serviced. A specified fuel quantity is shared in proportion to each selected tank’s free space; existing stores stay aboard their hulls. Fill tanks fills the selected ships. Each job uses the ordinary per-ship service charges and commits together, so insufficient funds never leave a partly serviced fleet.

Ammunition is listed openly by fitted weapon type. Load 1 each adds one round to every selected ship with compatible free space; Load max loads as much as the magazines and cargo holds allow. Quotes show the exact total rounds and price. Unsupported ammunition stays unavailable at that dock, and energy weapons require no ammunition.

Accounting lots, packaging and holds

Exchange prices and production recipes use lots of 250 kg of net material. A lot can contain many smaller packages: sacks, cases, drums, pressure cassettes or cryogenic dewars. The container is supplied with the lot and has no separate resale value. Loaded weight includes its tare; packed volume includes handling clearance. Both count when buying, loading freight or moving goods into storage. Dense metals usually reach the weight limit first; gases and delicate equipment can fill the hold before reaching that weight.

Each ship has a gross cargo weight limit and a separate volume limit suited to its role. Cargo fittings expand both; other fittings and ammunition consume some of that capacity. Spare parts use dedicated lockers and add to flight mass. Specialist handling rules still apply. Drive propellant stays in fuel tanks; packaged hydrogen and other exchange gases are merchandise. Fuel calculations use the actual gross shipment weight once. Raw-ore extraction leaves a small weight reserve for the cases used after refining. A warehouse bay holds 10 tonnes and 40 cubic metres, including reserved tenant cargo and stored equipment.

Goods lists and the bonding-curve tool show generic packaging specifications. Fill this hold considers both dimensions for the selected good and ship. Public prices remain per net lot; packaging does not change factory ingredient ratios. Existing excess cargo is retained for unloading, but further loading and departure require a valid fit.

Hull batches and reservations

Qualified basic hulls—Swift, Kestrel, Heron and Peregrine—have guaranteed, finite production batches at their supplying registries. Specialist classes retain variable availability. If a basic class sells out, reserve one hull from its next scheduled batch. Only one reservation may be held; no deposit is taken, and the full catalogue price is due when you collect it. A ready reservation survives travel and reloads. Cancelling it yields no money, goods or experience.

Fleets, upgrades and raids

Fleet lets you buy up to 32 owned ships, fit nine kinds of equipment, service each hull and switch command at the same station. Cargo and modules belong to individual ships. Assigned escorts travel on the same physical route and need their own propellant; berthed reserves stay at their ports and cost 1–5 cr per day according to the station; home-base reserves are free. Daily reserve fees use deposits at that station’s bank first, then local credit. Overdue reserve auctions sell the lowest-value berthed ships first; any surplus becomes a local bank deposit. Emergency recovery charges retain their ordinary cash/debt terms.

Pirate encounter risk grows with distance from Earth, with extra exposure on asteroid-belt routes. Patrol offices offer seven notorious-raider warrants. Accept a bounty in conversation to put it on your record and receive the patrol’s latest target intelligence. Acceptance is free and never launches combat. Accepted warrants remain open until capture; location information can be refreshed when it becomes stale. Normal travel near a reported station raises encounter odds; proximity and report freshness matter, and reports fade over 120 days. Each named target pays once: capture secures the warrant, then Claim bounty at a patrol office settles the payment. Legacy already-paid warrants retain their settlement. Every ship includes a standard laser; optional coilguns need ammunition. Battles open paused for fleet and individual orders. Live command lets both sides commit fire in the same exchange; each ship has one firing, maneuver or support opportunity. Earlier saved manual rounds keep their original controls until a clean round boundary. Salvos use ammunition and build heat; guard cools weapons, spares repair damage, and maneuvers use propellant. Withdraw or request emergency recovery if needed. Ten-minute turns and range bands are tactical abstractions, not an orbital combat simulator. A hull reduced to zero integrity is destroyed with its cargo, fuel and fitted equipment. Command transfers to a surviving escort. Voluntary surrender recovers intact hulls. Battle saves preserve the exact turn.

Live fleet command

Every finished battle opens a summary with the outcome, payment and fleet condition. Closing it resumes the remaining journey and docking checks; arrival opens the main station page. A failed blockade still prevents docking, and a destroyed travelling fleet proceeds to crew recovery. The summary is saved, so reloading before closing it cannot repeat the battle payment or skip the unfinished journey.

Ordinary raids use finite regional formations selected from overlapping threat bands based on the factory capability of your flagship class. An untouched courier on Earth’s local routes faces a single armed courier, rather than a combat cutter and escort. Larger flagship classes gradually encounter more capable groups. Adding or improving escorts does not immediately strengthen an ordinary contact; fitting upgrades remain a combat advantage; distant regions retain stronger minimum forces. Cash and cargo value do not increase enemy fittings, and damage or empty magazines do not make the next opponent weaker. Remote berthed ships do not provide protection. Enemy hulls begin intact, with real fitted weapons and finite ammunition.

Unaccepted named raiders enter ordinary travel only after the captain has some voyage or combat experience and an equipped formation suited to the group. Accepting a warrant deliberately seeks its named opponent even if you are unprepared; the target and posted bounty are not weakened. Check the contact’s readiness warning before engaging. That estimate considers standing roles, preferred weapons and range, ammunition reserves, current hulls, shields, thermal cycling and available repair supplies. Net lots and loaded holds

Prices and recipes use 250 kg of net material per lot. Packaging adds product-specific tare and packed volume. Cases, sacks, pressure cassettes and cryogenic dewars have different loads. Each ship and warehouse checks both gross weight and volume, plus specialist handling. Fuel uses the packaged shipment mass once. Containers are supplied with cargo and cannot be sold separately. The stock and price tool can fill a selected hypothetical ship using both limits.

Trade can close a gap between stations

Buying from a cheap station reduces its reserves and raises its later prices. Selling at an expensive station increases its reserves and lowers its later prices. Moving enough real supply can therefore narrow or close that good’s price gap. Production, consumption, seasonal plans and replenishment can open another opportunity later.

You can hold your own goods and wait for a better bid. The outcome still depends on the quantity you sell, travel time, compatible cargo space, access, propellant, provisions, berth charges, loan interest and losses. An immediate local buy-and-resell crosses the spread and the same marginal stock curve.

Recovery uses real feedstock and spare capacity

Ordinary batches receive ingredients before overtime is allocated. Shared input reserves can protect up to five days of local use when the receiving station has better coverage. That protection can pause an export; it cannot create goods or erase a route’s travel time.

When output stock falls below half its reference, non-collection lines can use up to 25% spare processing capacity. Every ingredient must already be present. Station power and workshop wear are metered, with overtime billed at 1.5 times their normal service cost. Critical input reserves consider both source and destination coverage. Primary collection remains on its normal tier curve. Concentrate refining returns useful coproducts and records residual waste, instead of destroying finished alloy.

Station stores have a separate limit for each good: the peak planned seasonal inventory plus 25% headroom, with a 100-lot minimum. Production eases above 90% committed occupancy and stops when any coproduct cannot fit. Incoming cargo waits rather than disappearing; new shipments reserve receiving room. Exchange sales stop at available storage space. Historical excess is retained while local use and outgoing trade work it down.

Freight uses real goods and scales with your fleet

Freight acceptance removes every product from the source exchange’s available stock and loads it into compatible active holds. At handover, the receiving client consumes the consignment; it is not added to destination exchange stock for resale. Where the product has local final-use demand, delivery offsets part of that later demand to prevent a second consumption charge. Pure factory feedstock consumed by a private client does not become public factory input or an unspendable final-use credit.

Higher tiers require earned Navigation or Commerce levels, licensed stations, total active cargo capacity and the listed number of active ships. The actual free, compatible holds and source stock are checked again when accepting. Products also retain their discovery and handling requirements.

A share of station profit, plus recorded travel costs

Client goods cost the captain nothing and remain sealed client property. The station values its acquisition at the source bid and its receiving invoice at the destination ask for the full mixed lot. After reserving the loaded reference hull’s operating budget, the remainder funds the commission. Offers need a positive commercial margin; the cargo’s replacement value does not create a fee.

The captain’s commission starts with an 8% share rate, with up to 10 percentage points for raid risk, 4 for receiving need, 2 for journey length and 1 for mixed-cargo handling. Existing crew, relic and captain benefits improve that rate before its cap. The formula leaves the station its retained profit and limits the captain’s commission to 25% of that retained profit. Raid risk is the same corridor-and-approach forecast used by navigation, including confirmed blockades. Higher risk cannot bypass the cap.

Travel reimbursement is separate: actual recorded fuel burn, provisions and berth costs are shared across cargo carried on each departure and capped at the quoted operating allowance. The same voyage cost cannot be reimbursed once for every job. Empty travel and jobs completed without recorded travel earn no travel reimbursement. Noncash capital, unrelated repairs, delays and unagreed costs are not additional allowances. The board shows the commission and expense ceiling, with the current amount payable on delivery. Accepted commissions and ceilings do not reprice. An unaccepted offer’s deadline is fixed from its dispatch issue date and loaded reference-route time, with 90 days of handling allowance. It disappears when the current loaded fleet’s flight plus clearance would arrive too late. Acceptance checks that timing again. Arrival on the due date is allowed; elapsed travel and incident delays count toward expiry. A new dispatch cycle can post a new offer, with its own deadline.

A missed deadline, abandonment or loss forfeits payment. Surviving goods stay sealed on their carrying ships, with no owned purchase basis, trading access or collateral value. Return them to a freight office where the carrying ship is physically present. Ordinary breaches bill only missing goods and the signed breach fee. Later loss of return cargo bills only the extra missing value, with no second breach fee. Theft is explicit and bills full restitution plus the fee; only then do retained goods become tradable with restitution basis. Funds pay first and unpaid charges join the original charter account at Meridian. Deposits are not silently withdrawn. Every settlement is recorded once.

Liberated stations pay temporary recovery premiums

Travelling to a blockaded station forces the blockade encounter before docking, whether or not you accepted a liberation commission. Defeating the entire hostile formation frees the station.

For 30 game days after liberation, ordinary exchange sell prices receive a fading recovery premium. It is strongest for goods the station consumes more than it produces. Each recovery bid remains below the corresponding marginal ask, so buying locally and immediately reselling cannot turn the premium into a profit loop.

The premium also covers your owned warehouse goods sold through that exchange. Restricted goods, relics, ship sales and contract payments retain their separate rules. These conditional voyage premiums are explained here and excluded from the generic baseline price curves. See the recovery formula.

Price curves help put a quote in context

The price distributions collect daily purchase and sale quotes over the complete 1,440-day public pricing cycle. Each eligible station-day counts once. Buy observations come from stations listing the item; sell observations come from accepting exchanges. The two curves show quoted prices in credits, with separate means, medians, extrema and standard-deviation markers.

A low buy percentile identifies an unusually cheap public ask; a high sell percentile identifies an unusually high public bid. Holding goods does not give a guaranteed sale date or transaction at that price. The frequency of a quote is not the likelihood of a profitable journey.

Max gain/loss and average gain/loss are a separate comparison of independent purchase and sale quote pairs, before route costs. They do not assume a feasible itinerary or ordered purchase and sale dates. Return-column sorting changes credits versus percentages; price curves always use credits. Check the market and who owns the cargo

Station buys means you can sell your own goods to that station. A station that consumes a good accepts it, including factory ingredients it does not retail. Station sells means you can buy from its available stock. A station that produces a good lists that output, even if it consumes more than it makes. Retail imports and transit merchandise can also be offered. A net-consumption label does not forbid local production or sales.

The game uses Buy and Sell; reference prices marked You buy are what you pay, and You sell are what you receive. Buyer-only stations have a sell-to-station price but no purchase offer. A station with no consumption, production or retail route for a product does not quote a trade there; those hypothetical quotes are excluded from its distributions. Stock can temporarily run out, and your own discoveries, licences and compatible cargo space still apply. The Sell view shows only eligible goods in the selected fleet or local warehouse; it does not list things you do not own.

Discovery, operating qualifications, regional access and compatible holds govern what a captain can buy or carry in the game. The reference site shows every option and its requirements, regardless of a player’s progress.

Restricted dealers and visiting canteen factors have their own finite stock, buyer demand and availability. Discreet dealers apply 2%, 1% or 0.375% each side for Limited, Steady or Plentiful supply. Visiting cargo factors begin at 3% each side of reference value; restricted cargo uses the competing dealer’s midpoint when that desk is open. Protective local quote limits keep their asks above the competing local bid, and their bids below the lowest same-day local ask. Whole-credit rounding still applies. Cargo-screening packages, individual ammunition types, engineering spares, relics and recovered antiquities retain their own purchase, use and resale rules. They do not inherit the ordinary exchange daily-price distribution.

Warehouses hold local inventory; only your own eligible goods can be moved or sold. Client freight and tenant consignments remain client-owned. Restricted warehouse tenants reserve real space and can face inspection and seizure under their agreed concealment and liability terms. Read about warehouse leases.

Plan a trading business

New captains have ten general-cargo goods. Medical supplies remain available for care requests in small batches of two lots per station/day; an Industrial account opens wholesale quantities. Earned Commerce and exchange visits open materials, industrial, specialist and advanced accounts. Goods within each account have staggered Commerce requirements. New discoveries arrive at most 2 at a time, rather than as a whole account. After a release, earn another 120 Commerce XP or make a first visit to another station to reveal the next eligible pair. Each good’s own XP, account and access requirements still apply. Browsing, waiting and zero-XP trades do not release more goods; large rewards never reveal more than 2 in one action. Navigation helps exploration but cannot open a wholesale account by itself. Owned goods and client freight remain visible when account requirements change; generic reference sites list every good independently of player progress.

The Exchange handles Buy and Sell orders for your own goods, with an explicit quantity, inventory and total. Client shipments and their delivery contracts belong at the Freight office. Compare the actual loaded route and operating costs before departure; trade tips and earlier quotes are not guaranteed future sale prices.

Profitable supplies to an undersupplied customer can build a bounded business relationship through ordinary sales. Warehouses can buffer purchases and stagger sales, but cargo stays at its physical station and still faces daily buyer limits. Leasing spare space is supplemental business, never permission to sell tenant cargo.

Bulk hulls serve large or mixed manifests; refrigerated, radiation, tanker and workshop holds serve different certified cargo. Compare the useful load after fittings and ammunition, loaded fuel and operating expenses rather than nominal capacity alone. A larger ship does not guarantee a larger profit: an extra lot may exhaust a buyer or erase the spread. Merchant objectives track reliable local customers and a wider regional network.

Raiders assess cargo value, regional exposure and fleet protection. A valuable load can face a larger demand than a starter shipment. Payment, evasion and negotiation remain merchant responses; a confirmed station blockade still forces its encounter before docking. A forecast raid risk is a chance of an encounter, not an insurance payment.

Continuing personal and regional stories

After the original five personal chapters, five further arcs open at relationship ages of two, five, fifteen, thirty-five and seventy-five years. They concern adapting a failed beginning, sharing responsibility, repairing a promise, succession and preserving an honest account. Each arc has three beats, including a persistent choice and an outcome, with at least thirty days between beats. The first follow-on resolution can fulfil the original undertaking, revise it with the people affected, or release a plan that did not work. Later recollections retain that result and name the new concern rather than permanently repeating the original ambition. Relevant professional experience and restored personal trust still matter. These are character developments, not extra paid cargo contracts or automatic market events.

Regional anchor contacts keep nine different local series: Meridian’s shared arrivals, the Caloris workshop, Aphrodite’s closed-environment care, the Mars–Phobos acknowledgements, Belt custody, Jovian rescue records, Saturnian stewardship, the outer assembly’s delayed replies and Neptune’s reception history. Each names the person who holds the other account. Hear that person at the actual receiving desk before returning to close the hearing.

Regional periods begin at game years 0, 2, 5, 10, 20, 35, 50, 75 and 100. They have different authored disputes and later consequences. A hearing already begun has no expiry. If an unaccepted period passed while you were away, a dated archive explains its off-screen resolution; reading it cannot retrospectively choose for those residents. Later periods remain available. These social story periods do not change prices, bank quotes, production or travel safety.

Ask for a lasting inquiry contact to put the named desk in your record. Tasks shows that lead whenever an account is ready, and replaces it with the current named handover once you begin. It does not depend on catching a rare dealer’s brief visit. Rare dealers keep finite stock and their original intermittent visits. The larger story pool is finite: alternative choices and missed opportunities cannot all be experienced in one journey, while freight, market cycles and some jobs recur indefinitely.

Correspondence and professional mastery

Communications lists new personal accounts from known remote contacts with rapport of at least three and intact personal trust. A correspondent can send at most one new account per 180 days. A dated letter can continue an eligible personal chapter or record your explicit personal choice, without moving your ships, paying XP or settling a physical handover. Actual cargo, inspection, contract and custody work still needs its named location. The latest letter remains in the character’s compact record; the durable action history keeps the earlier letters.

Earned skill ranks four through seven open mentoring, independent assessment, cross-region coordination and stewardship undertakings. Each profession progresses in order. Register at Communications, complete rank minus two new relevant work records, hear the named receiving coordinator and return after at least thirty days. Commerce counts deliveries and twenty-lot trading units; Engineering counts repairs and completed studies; Diplomacy counts aid, negotiations and completed diplomatic mandates; Navigation counts newly recorded journeys with an award of at least eight after corridor repetition, before captain XP bonuses. Earlier work establishes eligibility but cannot fulfil a new undertaking. These distinctions have no deadline, cash, free XP, material reward or automatic destination access.

Bounties

Accept a posted bounty at a patrol office. The warrant remains accepted while you travel; fresh target information comes from conversations with patrol staff or people in canteens. Their reports are dated and unconfirmed. Capturing the target secures the warrant and records the agreed payment. Return to any patrol office and choose Claim bounty to collect it once. Deposits, loans and interest

How rates are calculated · Station rate ranges

Cooperatives show your branch balances, annual rates, allowance and available new credit. Use Borrow floating or Borrow fixed to take a loan at that quoted rate. This choice applies only to the new draw. Floating credit and deposits adjust every ship-day; fixed credit locks the quoted annual rate for the life of that draw. The fixed quote is today’s floating rate plus a disclosed 1.5–2.5 percentage points, reflecting regional and import-financing risk, plus branch size and resupply time. Larger, well-supplied branches usually charge less to lock a rate; small, remote branches charge more. The local premium is sampled daily as import demand changes. This is an annual-rate premium, not an upfront fee. Later fixed draws use their own current quote and never enlarge an old cheap agreement. Early repayment has no fee. Repayments clear the highest current annual rate first.

Public rates combine a slow 1,440-day funding cycle (48 rate periods) and a shorter 330-day cycle (11 periods), plus branch risk, seasonal goods production versus consumption, and profitable gross exporter-to-consumer quote spreads. Public production, consumption and exporter-to-consumer quotes are sampled every ship-day. Those conditions change the rate’s acceleration: momentum builds, eases or reverses before moving the quoted rate. Damping and daily speed/acceleration limits prevent sharp jumps. The deposit-to-floating-loan spread widens in proportion to upward momentum and narrows in proportion to downward momentum; stronger movement has a larger effect. A cross-branch deposit ceiling prevents immediate borrow-and-redeposit profit. The station-specific premium for a new fixed loan remains separate. Quotes are unchanged within a ship-day, and very small changes may be hidden by display rounding. The economic target starts from the common funding rate and smaller regional/local adjustments, with a 1.2% floating-loan floor. The actual rate follows that target gradually. The lending spread uses the smoothed local baseline, adjusted up or down with rate momentum. The savings rate is the annual interest the bank pays you; the floating and fixed loan rates are the annual interest you pay the bank. A net-consuming station needs import finance; a producer may need export working capital. The same wholesale goods and seasonal model used by the exchange supplies these signals. Player trades, cash movements, reputation and refreshing a screen cannot reroll rates. Reputation and earned experience still affect credit availability.

Interest compounds over elapsed ship-days, including travel, work and clinic stays, using each daily quote actually crossed: balance × (1 + annual rate)^(days / 365.25). Fractional balances are retained; transactions use whole credits. No interest accrues merely by opening a page. Existing fixed loans retain their exact agreed rate. Carry opportunities can arise when an earlier fixed loan costs less than a later deposit rate, but later floating returns can fall. Compare the entire holding period, interest paid during travel, fuel, provisions, berth fees, other liabilities and risks. Goods arbitrage uses the actual purchase and sale quotes, finite stock, hold compatibility and ownership. Gross price spreads are a funding-demand signal, not a guaranteed return.

The banking chain is daily public economic conditions → rate targets → acceleration and velocity → quoted rates → interest. The target guides the direction of movement; the quoted rate follows gradually.

  1. The game day sets the common funding cycles.
  2. Seasonal goods production, consumption and public exchange prices supply local import pressure and export-financing demand.
  3. Regional banking risk and location adjust the station’s targets.
  4. A damped rate motor changes acceleration, then velocity, then the rate.
  5. Momentum widens or narrows the deposit-to-floating-loan spread. A separate station premium sets new fixed-loan quotes.
  6. Balances compound using each elapsed day’s applicable annual rate.

Rates below use decimals: 0.04 means 4%. “Clamp” keeps a value between its lower and upper limits.

Funding and local economic targets

F = 0.026 + 0.020 × sin(2π × day / 1440)
     + 0.006 × sin(2π × day / 330)
Balance = average[(production − consumption)
         / max(1, production + consumption)]
C = max(0, −Balance)
T = sum[min(1, best positive export margin)]
  / max(1, number of surplus goods)
Export margin = (consumer bid − exporter ask) / exporter ask

C measures net-consumption pressure across listed goods. T measures export working-capital demand using the best positive gross price gap to a consuming station. These price gaps exclude transport costs. R is the region’s banking-risk baseline above Earth; O is the location adjustment: 0.003 for the Moon, 0.001 for Earth high orbit, 0.0015 for other moons, and zero elsewhere.

Loan target = max(0.012,
 F + 0.010 + 0.4R + 0.5O + 0.003C + 0.002T)
Deposit target = clamp(
 F − 0.006 + 0.2R + O + 0.005C + 0.003T,
 0.002, F + 0.008)
Baseline spread target = Loan target − Deposit target

Acceleration, velocity and the lending spread

a = clamp(0.0144 × (target − rate) − 0.24 × velocity,
     −0.000025, +0.000025)
New velocity = clamp(velocity + a, −0.00035, +0.00035)
New rate = rate + new velocity
Momentum = clamp(New velocity / 0.00035, −1, +1)
Lending spread = clamp(
 smoothed baseline spread × (1 + 0.35 × Momentum),
 0.008, 0.040)
Floating quote = round4(max(0.012, new rate))
Deposit quote = round4(max(0.002, Floating quote − Lending spread))

The same damped update smooths the baseline spread. A falling target first slows an existing rise before it can reverse the rate. At full upward momentum, a 2-percentage-point baseline spread becomes 2.7 points; at full downward momentum, it becomes 1.3 points, before floors and the network ceiling apply. The final deposit quote also stays at least 0.1 percentage point below the cheapest current floating quote anywhere in the network.

Internal rate velocity is limited to 0.035 percentage points per ship-day, and acceleration to 0.0025 percentage points per ship-day squared. Quotes round to 0.01 percentage point, so a small daily change can leave the displayed number unchanged. Each date is reconstructed from at most 240 preceding daily targets, initialized at the first target with zero velocity; no growing rate-history file is added to a voyage.

Fixed borrowing and interest

L = clamp((resupply lead days − 24) / 86, 0, 1)
S = clamp((22 − station size) / 19, 0, 1)
Fixed premium = round4(0.015 + 0.003 × min(1, R / 0.062)
           + 0.002C + 0.0025L + 0.0025S)
New fixed quote = round4(Floating quote + Fixed premium)
Daily balance multiplier = (1 + annual rate)^(1 / 365.25)

The station-specific locking premium is 1.5–2.5 percentage points annually. A fixed loan keeps its signed rate; deposits and floating loans use the quote for every elapsed day. Long voyages multiply the applicable daily factors. Earlier recorded balances and outcomes are preserved.

Regional banking baselines, station size and resupply settings remain fixed profiles; the goods and funding signals are sampled daily. Public goods conditions influence banks. Bank rates do not currently change goods production, consumption or prices. Player trade perturbations and screen refreshes cannot reroll public quotes. Reputation, earned experience, assets and existing debt feed into borrowing limits.

Bank credit and enforcement

Each station bank has its own base credit budget. Your current limit grows with earned experience and improves with wider and local reputation; poor reputation can reduce it. Relic bonuses do not count as earned experience. Review your current allowance at the local cooperative. New loans also share one network assessment: every outstanding balance, including the charter and restitution debt, is counted against a record-based lending ceiling subject to earned-income and seasoning requirements, and supported at most by 65% of recoverable hull and fitting values, 50% of eligible cargo within remaining funded local buyer allowances, and 50% of unborrowed cash and deposits. Equity includes cash, deposits at every bank, owned legal cargo at its physical station’s conservative batch liquidation value, hulls at 75% of catalogue price adjusted for integrity, and fittings at 50% of installed cost. Travelling ships, berthed ships and goods in every warehouse are included. Warehouse bays, client shipments, restricted goods, unmarketed mission items, fuel, ammunition and spares do not support this estimate. Borrowed cash is offset by its matching debt, so moving loans or deposits between banks does not create more equity. The local branch ceiling still applies. Asset or price changes affect new loan availability; this assessment alone does not call in an existing loan or seize remote assets. Earth also handles the original charter and restitution balance. Arrival authorities collect cash for an over-limit local balance, targeting one credit below that arrival’s assessed limit. If cash leaves more than 110% of that limit owed, owned legal cargo, optional upgrades and local ships may be auctioned in that order. Surplus proceeds return to cash. Arrival collection leaves deposits and remote ships separate. Chargeable reserve berths are billed daily at their own station: local deposits first, then branch credit. Above 110% of that branch’s current limit, the lowest-value berthed ships are auctioned until its debt is cleared or those ships run out. Proceeds pay that branch’s credit first, including any charter balance held there; surplus is deposited at that bank. These reviews also run while you are elsewhere. Client freight is not sold as your property; losing its carrying hull triggers the ordinary freight-breach terms. More than 10% over the limit also increases normal raid risk near the creditor, with the projected effect shown in the flight plan. If every ship is seized, begin a new voyage.

Home base and station reputation

Every new captain starts at Meridian Station above Earth, their first home base. Register a different home while docked at its port authority for 2,500 credits; wait 30 days between moves. Reserve berth rates vary by station from 1 to 5 cr per ship per day; see the selected ship’s station and daily fee in Fleet overview. Home reserve berths are free and home clinic care and departure briefings receive a 20% discount. Your wider reputation follows you, while each station keeps its own record of deliveries, aid, dialogue and misconduct. Good standing can improve service quotes and personal introductions; poor standing can worsen them. Moving home never resets reputation, moves ships or clears debt.

Captain & records

Character growth and conversations

Your Captain’s record tracks your career. Establishment conversations branch into follow-up questions and can return to earlier subjects. Contacts may offer personal commissions, introductions or further work. An assignment identifies what is needed, where to bring it, its deadline and its payment or promised outcome. Tasks lists active commitments; the Journal records actions and outcomes.

Journal & action records

The Journal follows the saved action history: every completed action has its own dated row and matching record number. Notices and material results appear as separate compact rows under that record. Rows identify the action or event, its location and relevant person, goods, payment or outcome. Long messages continue on short rows; hover for the complete original text. Earned XP, levels, account movements and material inventory changes use actual saved differences, never promised rewards. Dates on notices identify when they occurred during a multi-day action. Conversations record the selected topic and any actual results without copying the full conversation tree.

Earlier entries load as you scroll. Older detail is reconstructed from adjacent saved action outcomes; opening the Journal never repeats an action or grants rewards. Restoring a save is identified as a change of recorded state. Original snapshots remain unchanged. Details from before the action ledger began can only be shown where they were actually recorded.

Reputation, stations & rumours

Good deeds build saved progress rather than awarding their old full score immediately. Higher reputation takes progressively more work. Trust at the station where you act strengthens its public endorsement; your wider reputation in turn affects local reception, service quotes, bank credit and customs checks. Local trust also builds more slowly. Setbacks apply immediately and erase progress toward the next point.

Exceeding 90 requires local trust 20 at three stations; exceeding 97 requires trust 30 at six; reaching 100 requires trust 40 at ten. Existing scores are retained. Ask a canteen or commons contact what people are saying about your reputation. Recent recorded deeds can introduce you elsewhere; misconduct spreads warnings. Each account reaches at most three other stations, once per station, within 180 days. Each station considers one account per 30 days. Good accounts build progress; bad ones cost up to two local trust and one wider reputation. Gossip cannot generate more gossip, and old praise cannot cover newer misconduct. Buying drinks grants no reputation.

World & science

The Asterion age

The magnetic-nozzle breakthrough of 2074 became a crewed drive in 2081 and a common retrofit under the Open Drive Compact in 2085. Every ship uses an Asterion fusion drive. The Engine story traces its development and adoption across the station network.

Government & treaties

Governments & treaties

A system of local governments, shared obligations and negotiated belonging.

2038 · The launch states

Earth’s national governments and regional unions still controlled launch licences, while their orbital facilities used separate rules for rescue and cargo liability. The Orbital Service Charter made a distress call a shared obligation and required every dock to name a civil authority; it did not create a government for the whole planet.

2051 · People who stayed

Lunar workers won elected habitat councils after remote owners tried to suspend water deliveries during a contract dispute. The Shackleton Articles separated life-support access from employment and recognised independent synthetic citizens as persons, establishing the principle that a resident’s rights could not belong to the company operating their berth.

2064 · A system of neighbours

Martian settlements formed a council for surface supplies and orbital arrivals, while Belt habitats pooled rescue ships without surrendering their own assemblies. The Jovian and Saturnian settlements later adopted mutual-aid leagues because instructions from Earth could arrive too late to settle a failing pump, a missing shipment or an evacuation.

2079 · The Interplanetary Compact

The Oriole rescue crisis and the first Asterion tests exposed the limits of separate emergency agreements. The Interplanetary Compact gave participating governments a joint assembly for rescue obligations, traffic standards and cross-border mediation; Earth’s launch administrations, lunar commons, Martian councils and habitat leagues kept their own civil law and budgets.

2083 · The Neptune Reception Covenant

The ark arriving beyond Neptune carried both Veyr and Ilyri communities after generations of sublight travel. A provisional covenant guaranteed emergency supplies, interpreters and the right to refuse settlement or biological examination, while elected ark delegates negotiated with human governments and synthetic citizens rather than surrendering their passengers to a single host state.

2084 · The Veyr Transit Accord

Veyr route houses and convoy assemblies negotiated recognition of shipboard civic records, translated manifests and crew representation in port hearings. In return, signatory crews accepted declared armaments, traceable cargo ownership and the receiving habitat’s safety rules; an oath between route houses could support testimony but could not replace evidence or a local access licence.

2085 · The Open Drive Compact

The manufacturing agreement described in the Asterion records exchanged retrofit rights for common fittings and safety standards. It gave old couriers a place in the new trade network, but remained a technical agreement distinct from the Interplanetary Compact and the later three-peoples settlement.

2086 · The Ilyri Habitat & Exchange Convention

Ilyri habitat circles and memory custodians sought control over living samples, medical consent and the records inherited from the ark. The convention established sealed biological cargo protocols, reciprocal habitat inspection by consent and stewardship claims for living collections; ownership of a sample did not grant ownership of the people or histories associated with it.

2087 · The Three Peoples Concord

Human government delegates, Veyr assemblies and Ilyri circles ratified equal civil standing for their residents, with synthetic citizens represented through their existing councils rather than assigned to a biological people. The Concord created a standing translation secretariat and mixed arbitration panels, while protecting each community’s choice of representatives and each habitat’s local jurisdiction.

2088 · The Arrival & Arbitration Protocol

A contested seizure exposed how easily a translated cargo declaration could become an accusation. The protocol required a named receiving authority, a dated record and access to an interpreter or mixed panel in treaty disputes; it also confirmed that access qualifications, customs restrictions and lawful local debt proceedings remained matters for the receiving port.

2089 · The desk between governments

Port authorities now serve as the Compact’s local liaison offices alongside their licensing and customs duties. Residents bring questions about which council can hear a dispute, what a treaty recognises and which office holds the evidence; independent captains carry that network’s daily promises between settlements that still disagree about power, resources and the cost of mutual aid.

Resources and settlement

Your charter begins in 2089, within a network of 26 stations. Gas and ice giants have no solid surface ports. You always dock in space. Ice-rich moons support processed-water exports where local facilities are available; water abundance alone does not provide cheap hydrogen fuel. Europa and Enceladus focus on research; their industries do not mine the subsurface oceans. Food, medicines and electronics require industrial supply chains.
